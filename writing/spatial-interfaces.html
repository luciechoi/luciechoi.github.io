The Spatial Dimension in Everyday Computing

July 2026 • Spatial Computing • Lucie Choi

Flat 2D interfaces have dominated computing for decades. Bringing subtle 3D spatial awareness into web experiences allows software to feel tangible, tactile, and natural without becoming cluttered.

"Spatial dimension does not mean excessive complexity — it means introducing depth, perspective, and lighting that resonate with human intuition."

Why Depth Matters

Our brains are wired to understand physical space: objects that are closer feel more immediate; shadows give cues about hierarchy; motion and inertia tell us about weight and responsiveness. When we weave these physical principles into digital interfaces with real-time WebGL rendering, computing becomes more delightful and intuitive.

The Future of the Spatial Web

As graphics performance in browsers improves and spatial displays become more common, the boundary between digital and physical design will continue to blur. The challenge for software creators is to harness this power thoughtfully — serving clarity above novelty.
