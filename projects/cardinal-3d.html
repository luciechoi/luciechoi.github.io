Overview

A 3D modeling and rendering engine written in C++ for Stanford CS248A (Computer Graphics: Animation & Rendering). The project is composed of two primary modules:

  • MeshEdit: A discrete differential geometry engine based on a halfedge data structure, supporting local topological mutations, Catmull-Clark subdivision, isotropic remeshing, and Garland-Heckbert quadric error surface simplification.
  • PathTracer: An unbiased Monte Carlo ray tracer evaluating the Rendering Equation with Binned SAH BVH spatial acceleration, Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS), and physically based BSDF models.

1. Geometry Processing & Mesh Editing

Halfedge Mesh Topology & Local Operations

Polygon meshes are represented using a pointer-based Halfedge Data Structure (Halfedge_Mesh). Each directed halfedge points to its next(), twin(), vertex(), edge(), and incident face(), allowing \(O(1)\) local neighborhood queries (such as circulating a vertex 1-ring star) while strictly maintaining 2-manifold surface properties.

  • Edge Split (split_edge): Inserts a midpoint vertex along an edge and creates two internal edges, subdividing two incident triangles into four smaller triangles.
  • Edge Flip (flip_edge): Re-orients the shared diagonal edge between two adjacent triangles across their bounding quad, preserving manifold invariants.
  • Edge Collapse (collapse_edge): Merges endpoint vertices into a centroid while checking topological guards to avoid face inversion or non-manifold vertices.
  • Face / Vertex / Edge Bevel: Extrudes polygons along normals and tangents to produce chamfered edges, geometric insets, and paneling.

Subdivision & Isotropic Remeshing

  • Catmull-Clark Subdivision: Recursively computes face, edge, and vertex points to produce smooth, curvature-continuous limit surfaces.
  • Isotropic Remeshing: Iteratively optimizes mesh triangles toward regular valence-6 equilateral elements via edge splitting (\(> \frac{4}{3}L\)), collapsing (\(< \frac{4}{5}L\)), flipping, and tangential Laplacian smoothing.

Quadric Error Simplification (Garland-Heckbert)

To decimate dense triangle meshes while preserving geometric features and silhouettes, the engine implements Garland-Heckbert Quadric Error Simplification (QEM):

  • Fundamental Quadrics: For each triangular face plane \(\mathbf{p} = [a, b, c, d]^T\), the distance squared to a point \(\mathbf{v}\) is \(\mathbf{v}^T Q \mathbf{v}\), where \(Q = \mathbf{p} \mathbf{p}^T\).
  • Optimal Collapse Position: Solves the linear system \(\nabla E(\mathbf{v}^*) = 0\) for the lowest-error vertex position \(\mathbf{v}^*\), falling back to endpoints and midpoints when ill-conditioned.
  • Priority Queue Decimation: Candidate edge collapses are queued in a min-heap by error cost, iteratively simplifying the mesh to the target polygon budget.
Interactive isotropic remeshing: dynamically optimizing mesh quality and triangle valences via iterative edge splits, collapses, flips, and tangential Laplacian smoothing.

2. Physically Based CPU Path Tracer

The Rendering Equation & Light Transport

The path tracing module simulates global light transport by numerically evaluating Kajiya's Rendering Equation:

$$L_o(p, \omega_o) = L_e(p, \omega_o) + \int_{\Omega} f_r(p, \omega_i, \omega_o) L_i(p, \omega_i) (\omega_i \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, d\omega_i$$

Rays are recursively shot from the camera. At each intersection \(p\), emitted radiance \(L_e\) is combined with incoming radiance \(L_i\) integrated over the hemisphere \(\Omega\) weighted by the BSDF \(f_r\) and cosine foreshortening \((\omega_i \cdot \mathbf{n})\).

Spatial Acceleration: Binned SAH BVH

  • Binned Surface Area Heuristic: Primitives are partitioned into 32 spatial bins per axis to minimize the SAH cost metric:

    $$\text{Cost}_{\text{SAH}} = C_{\text{trav}} + \frac{S_A}{S} N_A C_{\text{isect}} + \frac{S_B}{S} N_B C_{\text{isect}}$$

    accelerating ray-primitive intersection from \(O(N)\) to \(O(\log N)\).
  • Möller-Trumbore Ray-Triangle Intersection: Fast vectorized barycentric coordinate ray-triangle intersection with bounding box pruning.
Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH) spatial acceleration structure constructed with Binned Surface Area Heuristic (SAH) partitioning for \(O(\log N)\) ray-primitive intersection.

Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS)

To eliminate stochastic Monte Carlo fireflies and high variance near small light sources, the renderer blends direct light sampling with BSDF importance sampling using the Veach Balance Heuristic:

Physically Based BSDF Models

  • Lambertian Diffuse: Perfectly diffuse reflection sampled via cosine-weighted hemisphere distribution.
  • Ideal Specular (Mirror): Delta reflection along the specular direction \(\omega_r = -\omega_o + 2(\omega_o \cdot \mathbf{n})\mathbf{n}\).
  • Refractive Dielectric (Glass): Transparent optics modeling Snell's law refraction, total internal reflection (TIR), and Schlick's Fresnel reflectance approximation.
  • Cook-Torrance Microfacet: Rough conductor/dielectric reflection using GGX normal distribution function \(D(\mathbf{h})\) and Smith geometric shadowing-masking \(G(\omega_o, \omega_i)\).
