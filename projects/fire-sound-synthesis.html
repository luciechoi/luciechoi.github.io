Overview & Acoustic Physics of Fire

Unlike rigid-body impact sound or modal vibrations of solid shells, open flame combustion sound is governed by unsteady volumetric expansion. When combustible fuel-air gases enter the flame front, rapid chemical reactions dramatically elevate temperature, causing the gas volume to violently expand.

Following the acoustic formulation by Chadwick et al. [1], the far-field acoustic pressure \(p(t)\) emitted by a compact flame monopole source is directly proportional to the time derivative of the total volumetric expansion flux across the flame reaction isosurface \(S(t)\):

$$p(t) = \frac{d}{dt} \int_{S(t)} \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t) \cdot \mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x}, t) \, dS(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{d}{dt} I(t)$$

• \(S(t)\): Dynamic flame reaction isosurface bounding the combustion front.
• \(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)\): Gas fluid velocity field at position \(\mathbf{x}\) and time \(t\).
• \(\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x}, t)\): Outward unit normal vector on the flame front surface element \(dS\).
• \(I(t) = \int_{S(t)} \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS\): Instantaneous volumetric expansion flux integral.
• \(p(t) = \frac{d}{dt} I(t)\): Acoustic pressure wave emitted by the flame.

Because macroscopic fluid solvers cannot efficiently resolve high-frequency turbulent acoustics at 44.1 kHz, this project adopts a two-scale synthesis strategy:

  1. Low-Frequency Roar (\(< 120\text{ Hz}\)): Evaluated directly from the physical expansion flux integral \(I(t)\) of the Houdini Pyro simulation.
  2. High-Frequency Sizzle (\(120\text{ Hz} - 10\text{ kHz}\)): Synthesized via spectral bandwidth extension obeying Kolmogorov turbulent combustion scaling laws.

Houdini Simulation & Sound Data Preparation

1. Pyro Simulation Setup

I started by prototyping the combustion solver on a basic torch flame to establish and validate the data export pipeline before moving to complex typography. In Houdini's Pyro solver, the combustion reaction rate is represented by the scalar flame field.

2. Isosurface Flux Integral Calculation

To compute the surface integral \(\int_{S(t)} \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS\), the volume simulation is processed through custom SOP geometry nodes:

  • Convert Volume SOP: Extracts the dynamic flame reaction isosurface from the flame scalar field at a specified isovalue threshold.
  • Measure SOP: Computes the surface area \(dS\) of each polygon primitive on the extracted mesh.
  • Trilinear Velocity Sampling: Uses the VolumeSample VEX API to interpolate fluid velocity \(\mathbf{u}\) from the 3D velocity voxels to surface points.
  • Staircase Artifact Mitigation: Voxel discretizations create jagged normals on the isosurface. In early tests, these sharp normal discontinuities introduced artificial high-frequency noise spikes in \(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n}\). Applying an Attribute Blur SOP on vertex position, normal, and velocity fields smoothly resolved this issue without damping physical bulk motion.

3. Audio-Rate Sampling & Data Verification

The flux integral was simulated and recorded at 240 FPS (\(f_s^{\text{sim}} = 240\text{ Hz}\)), corresponding to a Nyquist frequency of \(f_{\text{Nyquist}} = 120\text{ Hz}\). The integral sequence \(I(t)\) was exported to CSV for python synthesis.

Physical Flux Curve Verification

The exported flux integral \(I(t)\) exhibits strong physical coherence: it rapidly ramps up from ignition as expanding gas envelopes the ignition points, subsequently settles into turbulent periodic oscillations, and occasionally reaches negative instantaneous flux values during rapid localized gas cooling and contraction.

Low-Frequency Sound Synthesis

The raw simulation flux data captures the deep, rumbling "roar" of the combustion process.

To reconstruct continuous 44.1 kHz audio from the 240 Hz sampled data:

  • Integral-Domain Upsampling: We apply cubic spline interpolation (scipy.interpolate.CubicSpline) directly to the flux integral \(I(t)\) rather than the finite-difference derivative \(\frac{dI}{dt}\). Interpolating before differentiation ensures a \(C^2\)-continuous pressure curve \(p(t) = \frac{d}{dt}I(t)\) without discrete derivative discretization noise.
  • Acoustic Pressure Evaluation: Central finite differences on the upsampled continuous flux produce the low-frequency audio signal (fire_sound_low.wav).

Spectral Bandwidth Extension

The Physics of Fire Hiss

While the low-frequency rumble captures bulk combustion dynamics, natural fire emits a rich, broad-band crackle and hiss spanning up to several kilohertz. According to turbulent combustion acoustics, the sound power spectrum of turbulent flame fronts empirically satisfies an inverse power law decay:

$$S(f) \propto f^{-\alpha/2}, \quad \alpha \in [2.2, 3.4]$$

The parameter \(\alpha\) dictates the spectral decay slope. Lower values (\(\alpha \approx 2.2\)) retain more high-frequency energy, yielding a sharp, crisp hiss, while higher values (\(\alpha \approx 3.4\)) produce a smoother, warmer timbre.

Short-Time Windowing & Synthesis Pipeline

Short-time windowed synthesis pipeline: low-frequency pressure \(p(t)\) is windowed and matched in energy with shaped noise \(N_W(t)\) following \(f^{-\alpha/2}\).

The synthesis algorithm processes the audio via an overlap-add Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) architecture:

  1. Windowing: The upsampled pressure signal is segmented into overlapping Hann windows \(w(t)\) to produce windowed frames \(p_W(t)\).
  2. Noise Generation & Spectral Shaping: White noise frames \(N_W(t)\) are generated, transformed to frequency domain, and shaped by the \(f^{-\alpha/2}\) power-law filter.
  3. Dynamic Energy Matching (\(\beta\) Scaling): To seamlessly attach the synthetic high-frequency spectrum to the physical low-frequency simulation, a scaling factor \(\beta\) is computed per frame:

    $$\beta = \frac{E_{\text{low}} \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Area}_{\text{high}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{low}}}\right)}{E_{\text{noise}}}$$

    where \(E_{\text{low}}\) is the low-frequency simulation energy in the frame, and \(E_{\text{noise}}\) is the shaped noise energy.
  4. Temporal Smoothing: A Gaussian blur filter is applied across consecutive \(\beta\) values to prevent audible energy fluttering between adjacent frames.
  5. Spectral Blending & Overlap-Add: The low-frequency spectrum and scaled noise spectrum are blended with smooth transition weights across the cutoff boundary (\(f_{\text{cut}} = 120\text{ Hz}\)), followed by Inverse FFT (IFFT) and overlap-add buffer accumulation.

Debugging & Implementation Insights

  • Issue 1 (Overpowering Hiss & Lost Low-Frequency Pattern): In early implementations, the high-frequency hiss overwhelmed the signal, masking the roar. This was caused by improper frequency band masking when combining low and high bands. Implementing smooth transition blend weights restored low-frequency clarity.
  • Issue 2 (Disappearing High Frequencies / Beta Collapse): After fixing Issue 1, high frequencies vanished entirely because \(\beta\) collapsed to \(\sim 10^{-15}\). Debugging revealed that evaluating the \(0\text{ Hz}\) DC bin with \((0 + \epsilon)^{-\alpha/2}\) produced a massive singularity that inflated the noise denominator. Excluding the 0 Hz DC bin from energy integration completely resolved the scaling bug.

Animation & Final Composition

Burning Text Simulation

For the final composition, "Undying Passion (2026)", a 3D typography geometry ("448Z") was modeled and set alight using progressive ignition spreading in Houdini Pyro SOP.

  • Cache Optimization: Because simulating at 240 FPS generated massive volume data, optimizations including OpenVDB sparse grids, 16-bit half-float compression, and tight bounding box empty space culling were employed, bringing the 5-second high-density cache to ~100 GB.
  • Audio-Video Alignment: The synthesized audio is aligned with the video at the tail end rather than frame 0. Because combustion begins with minimal flame surface at frame 0 and progressively consumes the typography, the sound volume naturally swells over time as the burning surface area increases.
  • Mantra & DaVinci Resolve: Rendered in Houdini Mantra with volumetric blackbody emission and composited in DaVinci Resolve for final audio mastering and color grading.
