Motivation

Self-love can be a difficult topic for many people. At least, it has been for me.

When I was going through a tough period in my life in 2019, I was fortunate to meet a warm-hearted person who helped me see the crux of the problem. She told me “to be kind to myself” — a concept I had never truly fathomed before. But deep down, I knew that was it — and that it was one of the hardest things I struggled with and, indeed, what was creating a chain of more problems.

“I have hundreds of problems to solve, responsibilities to fulfill, and ambitions to chase. What does it even mean to be kind to myself?” I can buy nice things for myself, give myself ample space and time, cheer myself up that I am doing great and accept my flaws. Yet, it can still feel awkward and inauthentic.

Fast forward to 2025, I'm still answering that question. And here is the piece of how I picture loving and being kind to myself.

In a private room, only you and yourself. What conversation do you have with yourself?

Overview

This project, as part of the assignment for CS248B: Animation and Simulation, implements a 2D character animation system. It contains forward kinematics (FK), inverse kinematics (IK) with gradient descent optimization, weighted joint constraints, and different keyframe interpolation schemes.

1. Inverse & Forward Kinematics

The character is constructed as an articulated tree of transformation nodes (translations, hinges, and fixed limbs). In Forward Kinematics (FK), global joint positions are obtained by compounding local transformation matrices down the kinematic chain. The Inverse Kinematics (IK) subsystem allows users to attach interactive handles to any joint, numerically optimizing generalized joint coordinates \(\mathbf{q}\) to guide end-effectors to targets in real time.

Mathematical Formulation

Let \(\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^n\) represent the vector of generalized joint coordinates (translations and rotation angles), and let \(\mathbf{x}_i(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^2\) denote the global position of the \(i\)-th joint handle obtained via forward kinematics. For \(m\) active IK handles with target positions \(\mathbf{p}_i^*\), the objective function minimizes the squared Euclidean distance:

$$E(\mathbf{q}) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{m} \|\mathbf{x}_i(\mathbf{q}) - \mathbf{p}_i^*\|^2$$

The displacement rate of the end-effectors with respect to joint perturbations is given by the Jacobian matrix \(J(\mathbf{q}) = \frac{\partial \mathbf{x}}{\partial \mathbf{q}}\). The gradient of the objective function is:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{q}} E(\mathbf{q}) = J^T \big(\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{q}) - \mathbf{p}^*\big)$$

The optimization loop iteratively updates joint parameters using gradient descent with step size \(\alpha\):

$$\mathbf{q}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{q}^{(k)} - \alpha \nabla_{\mathbf{q}} E(\mathbf{q}^{(k)})$$

Multi-Handle Support & Robustness

  • Simultaneous Constraints: The solver supports at least 6 simultaneous IK handles without performance degradation.
  • Dynamic Handle Management: Handles can be attached to any joint (maximum 1 handle per joint) and removed in arbitrary order without resetting the active pose.

2. Modeling with Constraints & Coupled Mirror Kinematics

To animate the mirror reflection convincingly in a 2D plane while maintaining natural physical posture, the system couples character kinematics with weighted constraint optimization:

Pseudo-3D Perspective & Scale Transforms

Because the scene is rendered in 2D, the mirror counterpart shares the horizontal translation and joint rotations of the real character, but differs in vertical position (\(T_y\)) and depth scale (\(S\)). A scale transform is introduced into the kinematic chain to provide a faux-3D depth effect as the character moves toward or away from the mirror plane.

Projected IK Solver

Running independent gradient descent passes on both characters produces slight divergence in joint angles \(\mathbf{q}\), which breaks mirror symmetry and introduces computational lag. Instead:

  • Coordinate Frame Projection: All IK handles placed on the mirror reflection are projected into the real character's local coordinate frame.
  • Unified Pose Solve: A single gradient descent pass computes the optimal \(\mathbf{q}\), which is then mapped to both characters with appropriate translation and scale offsets. Moving the mirror character directly updates the real character and vice versa.
  • Scale Isolation: Scale is excluded from the IK optimization gradient so that characters do not unnaturally stretch or shrink to reach distant targets. However, scale remains an active DOF with non-zero weight during keyframe interpolation.

Weighted Constraints & Regularization

A diagonal weight matrix \(W\) regularizes the optimization to produce natural postures:

  • Vertical Translation Dampening: A small weight \(w_{\text{transY}}\) discourages excessive vertical drift during limb manipulation.
  • Upright Posture Prior: Moderate torso hinge stiffness prevents extreme slouching or unnatural back bends.
  • Joint Rotation Limits: Angular bounding constraints enforce anatomical limits on wrist and ankle rotations.

3. Keyframe Interpolation

Once poses of the character are keyframed, the system interpolates joint degrees of freedom across time. The visual smoothness and realism of the resulting movement depend on the interpolation scheme:

3-1. Linear Interpolation (\(C^0\) Continuity)

Linear interpolation simply blends linearly between consecutive keyframes. This gives \(C^0\) positional continuity, meaning the motion begins and ends at the exact given keys without any spatial jumps or gaps.

However, because the velocity changes abruptly at every keyframe — starting, stopping, or shifting directions instantly — the motion appears rigid, robotic, and mechanical. Natural human movement rarely speeds up or turns with sudden, instantaneous velocity spikes.

$$\mathbf{p}(u) = (1 - u)\,\mathbf{p}_i + u\,\mathbf{p}_{i+1}, \quad u \in [0, 1]$$

3-2. Catmull-Rom Splines (\(C^1\) Continuity)

Catmull-Rom splines construct smooth cubic curves that pass directly through every authored keyframe \(\mathbf{p}_i\). They provide \(C^1\) velocity continuity, meaning the speed and direction (the first derivative) transition smoothly across keyframes.

This eliminates the sharp corners of linear interpolation, allowing limbs to swing smoothly through poses without sudden jerkiness. For a normalized time parameter \(u \in [0, 1]\) between keyframes \(\mathbf{p}_i\) and \(\mathbf{p}_{i+1}\), each pose is evaluated via matrix multiplication:

$$\mathbf{p}(u) = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & u & u^2 & u^3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -5 & 4 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_{i-1} \\ \mathbf{p}_i \\ \mathbf{p}_{i+1} \\ \mathbf{p}_{i+2} \end{bmatrix}$$

Boundary conditions at the start and end of the timeline are handled with dedicated endpoint tangent extensions.

3-3. Uniform Cubic B-Splines (\(C^2\) Continuity)

Uniform cubic B-splines achieve even greater smoothness with \(C^2\) acceleration continuity. By ensuring that acceleration (the second derivative) changes continuously across boundaries, the motion completely eliminates sudden "jerk" — producing the softest, most organic and physically natural animation.

Clamped Endpoint Handling: Because standard B-splines treat keyframes as guiding de Boor control points rather than strictly passing through them, two duplicate keyframes are automatically inserted at the beginning and end (\([\mathbf{d}_0, \mathbf{d}_0, \dots, \mathbf{d}_n, \mathbf{d}_n]\)). This clamps the spline so the animation cleanly starts and stops at the exact intended poses.

$$\mathbf{p}(u) = \frac{1}{6} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & u & u^2 & u^3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 3 & -6 & 3 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{d}_{i-1} \\ \mathbf{d}_i \\ \mathbf{d}_{i+1} \\ \mathbf{d}_{i+2} \end{bmatrix}$$
