Motivation

Temples’ ambient sounds make me feel calm, so I often play them as background music. In this project, I aimed to simulate the sound of the most famous Korean temple bell, the Sacred Bell of King Seongdeok (성덕대왕신종), driving the audio from its structure and material properties. I also reproduced the sound from listeners’ relative hearing positions, where distance and angle from the bell matter, to best resemble the real physical experience.

Background

The Sacred Bell of King Seongdeok, made in 771 CE, has a big cultural and historical significance in Korea. Therefore, the bell has been closely studied, and many attempts were made to recreate the sound through scientific approaches. The regular bell striking ceremony has stopped in 1992 to prevent further damage to the artifact.

As heard in this recording, the sound exhibits several defining acoustic characteristics:

  • Two Main Frequencies: The hum tone (\(64\ \text{Hz}\)) and the fundamental tone (\(168\ \text{Hz}\)) make up the body of the sound. The fundamental tone has a shorter decay time around 20s, while the hum tone has a long decay up to 2 minutes.
  • The Beating Phenomenon (맥놀이): Subtle structural and thickness asymmetries create closely spaced pairs of eigenfrequencies, producing ringing sounds that fade in and out periodically. The hum tone has a 3s interval, while the fundamental tone has a 9s interval.
  • The Acoustic Sound Pipe (음통): A hollow pipe at the top is designed to absorb high frequencies above 300 Hz, leaving only a deep low-frequency reverberation.

The end goal was to match these characteristics as closely as possible, given the mesh and bell properties.

1. Modal Analysis using Finite Element Method (FEM)

The bell geometry was tetrahedralized to perform modal analysis. Using material parameters for a similar bronze bell (mass density \(\rho = 8800\ \text{kg/m}^3\), Poisson ratio \(\nu = 0.34\), and Young’s Modulus \(E = 79\ \text{GPa}\)), mass and stiffness matrices were assembled:

  • Mass Matrix (\(\mathbf{M}\)): Formed by integrating volume density per tetrahedron, distributing the element mass to the four vertices, and assembling into a global sparse matrix.
  • Stiffness Matrix (\(\mathbf{K}\)): Assembled from element stiffness matrices \(\mathbf{K}_e = \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{B} \cdot V\) for each tetrahedron:
    • Constitutive Matrix (\(\mathbf{C}\)): A \(6 \times 6\) elasticity matrix derived from Hooke’s law relating 3D stress to strain (\(\mathbf{\sigma} = \mathbf{C} \mathbf{\epsilon}\)) via Young’s Modulus (\(E\)) and Poisson’s ratio (\(\nu\)): $$\mathbf{C} = \frac{E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)} \begin{bmatrix} 1-\nu & \nu & \nu & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \nu & 1-\nu & \nu & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \nu & \nu & 1-\nu & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \frac{1-2\nu}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \frac{1-2\nu}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \frac{1-2\nu}{2} \end{bmatrix}$$
    • Strain-Displacement Matrix (\(\mathbf{B}\)): A \(6 \times 12\) matrix composed of four \(6 \times 3\) submatrices \(\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_1 & \mathbf{B}_2 & \mathbf{B}_3 & \mathbf{B}_4 \end{bmatrix}\) relating nodal displacements to internal strain (\(\mathbf{\epsilon} = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_e\)): $$\mathbf{B}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial x} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial y} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial z} \\ 0 & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial z} & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial z} & 0 & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial x} & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$ where \(\nabla N_i = \left(\frac{\partial N_i}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial N_i}{\partial z}\right)\) are the constant spatial gradients of the linear shape functions.
  • Generalized Eigenvalue Problem: Solved for natural frequencies \(f_i = \frac{\omega_i}{2\pi}\) and mode shapes \(\mathbf{\phi}_i\): $$\mathbf{K}\mathbf{\phi}_i = \omega_i^2 \mathbf{M}\mathbf{\phi}_i$$ The first six 0 Hz rigid-body modes were discarded to model the free-hanging pivot condition.

2. Modal Damping & Sound Synthesis

Because real bells exhibit frequency-dependent energy dissipation rather than uniform Rayleigh damping, individual damping ratios (\(\zeta_i\)) were calibrated from historical \(T_{60}\) reverberation measurements (\(e^{-\zeta \omega T_{60}} = 10^{-3}\)):

  • Hum Tone (64 Hz doublet): \(\Delta f \approx 0.34\ \text{Hz}\), \(\sim 3\ \text{s}\) beating interval, \(\zeta = 0.00013\), audible for over 60 seconds.
  • Fundamental Tone (168 Hz doublet): \(\Delta f \approx 0.13\ \text{Hz}\), \(\sim 9\ \text{s}\) beating interval, \(\zeta = 0.00030\), persisting for ~20 seconds.
  • Modal Superposition: Time-domain audio was synthesized by summing the decaying sinusoidal responses: $$y(t) = \sum_{i} A_i e^{-\zeta_i \omega_i t} \sin(\omega_i t + \theta_i)$$

3. Acoustic Transfer: BEM vs. Equivalent Source Method (ESM)

To simulate radiation into the surrounding 3D space, both BEM and ESM were evaluated:

  • BEM (bempp-cl): Tested Burton-Miller boundary integrals on the surface mesh (10K vertices / 60K triangles). While accurate, solving large dense matrices for high frequencies without dedicated GPU acceleration proved computationally prohibitive.
  • Equivalent Source Method (ESM): Placed proxy multipole sources inside the bell volume (offset by \(-5\text{ cm}\) along the surface normal). Constructed the transfer matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) using Green's function gradients and solved for source strengths with Tikhonov regularization. This yielded sound pressure levels (SPL) and listener-position gains across different azimuth angles (\(0^\circ, 15^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ\)).

4. Acoustic Radiation Visualization (Houdini & DaVinci Resolve)

The sound field was visualized in 3D using Houdini FX:

  • Particle emission and velocity vectors driven directly by synthesized audio amplitude curves via Houdini CHOPs.
  • Particles radiate outward in hemispherical pressure wavefronts around a centered 3D Bodhisattva statue, evoking tranquility and cultural heritage.
  • Final color grading, glowing luminance transitions, and precise audio-visual synchronization were composited in DaVinci Resolve.
